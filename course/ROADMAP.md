# Course Architecture and Learning Roadmap

## At a glance

| Item | Plan |
|---|---|
| Phases | 12 |
| Core lessons | 16 |
| Guided workshops | 4 embedded labs plus the capstone |
| Recommended deep dives | 3 full-video studies |
| Optional case library | 13 real-video analyses; select, do not binge |
| Final project | A production-ready long-form script package and an independent Short |
| Suggested pace | 6–10 weeks at 4–6 focused hours per week; slower is fine |
| Voiceover plan | 16 complete scripts (about 18,700 words): roughly 2:05–2:25 of continuous narration at 130–150 words per minute; guided demonstrations and learner pauses bring the recorded course toward 2.5–3 hours |

A phase is a skill milestone, not a page range. Move on when you can make the skill visible in your own writing—not when you have merely read the page.

## The learning journey

| Phase | Lesson(s) | Estimated study time* | Skill gained | Required practice / milestone |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **1. Foundations** | **1. From Topic to Video Idea**; **2. From Thought to Clear Sentence** | 2–3 h | Turn a broad subject into a specific viewer-facing question, promise, and clear paragraph. | One-page idea brief; five observation sentences; a 100-word explanation in plain spoken English. |
| **2. Scripting Mindset** | **3. Make the Writer’s Decisions** | 1.5–2 h | Decide what the viewer needs, what the evidence can support, what belongs now, and what to remove. | Decision log for a 60–90-second sequence; justify one cut and one early proof. |
| **3. Story and Structure** | **4. Build Movement from Cause and Choice**; **5. Choose a Shape That Fits the Material** | 2.5–3.5 h | Create change through desire, obstacle, new information, choice, and consequence; choose a structure for a reason. | Seven-beat map from a real event; two outlines of the same material, with a reason for each form. |
| **4. Ideas, Audience, and Research** | **6. Find the Question, Check the Claim, Build the Evidence Path** | 1.5–2.5 h | Evaluate ideas, research responsibly, distinguish fact/observation/inference/opinion, and organize evidence for learning. | Claim ledger with a counterpoint; research cards reordered into a five-to-eight-beat outline. |
| **5. Hooks and Retention** | **7. Earn Attention, Then Pay It Back** | 1.5–2 h | Write different honest openings, use open loops responsibly, and diagnose sections that do not change viewer understanding. | Hook workshop: three different contracts for one idea; mark the first proof and every loop closure. |
| **6. The Human Ear** | **8. Build a Human Voice**; **9. Write for Breath, Rhythm, and Comprehension** | 2–3 h | Replace generic language with specific observation; make English natural to say and easy to follow. | 200-word authenticity audit; record, annotate, and rewrite a 120–150-word read-aloud. |
| **7. Visual and Audio Thinking** | **10. Write for Image, Sound, and Edit** | 1.5–2 h | Make a production script where VO, visuals, sound, silence, and editing contribute distinct information. | Convert one 25-second beat into recording copy and editor copy; include one proof shot and one intentional hold. |
| **8. Long and Short Forms** | **11. Change the Architecture, Not Just the Runtime** | 1.5–2.5 h | Design a full short-form idea and a long-form investigation without speeding up or padding. | Build a self-contained 30–45-second Short and an 8–12-minute outline from related material. |
| **9. Professional Workflow** | **12. Brief-to-Script Workshop** | 2.5–4 h | Move from brief to research, angle, outline, draft, revision, and production-ready files with stop conditions. | Finish the workshop packet: brief, evidence map, beats, three hooks, draft, change log, recording/editor copies. |
| **10. Autopsy and Revision** | **13. Diagnose a Weak Script**; **14. Revise in the Right Order** | 2–3 h | Name the failure, explain its effect, make a truthful repair, and use evidence-based feedback. | Annotate a weak sample; deliver v1/v2 plus a ten-pass decision log and scorecard. |
| **11. Advanced Craft** | **15. Control the Lens, Emphasis, and Omission** | 1.5–2.5 h | Use perspective, counterargument, specificity, emotional progression, and restraint without overclaiming. | Write three different promises from the same verified material; select one and name what it must omit. |
| **12. Capstone** | **16. Build a Production-Ready Long Script and Short** | 5–8 h across the project | Work independently, test the audience contract, support claims, revise, and hand off a production package. | Long script (usually 6–12 minutes if earned), independent 25–60-second Short, source/rights log, revision history, production notes, postmortem. |

\*Study time includes reading, doing the exercise, and revising. The voiceover is not extra required reading; use it to learn, then make the artifact. Research depth and production access may change the schedule.

## Voiceover estimates by lesson

These are targets, not promises that every recording will fit a clock:

| Lesson | Planned spoken length | Why it gets that time |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. From Topic to Video Idea | 10–12 min | The distinctions are foundational and need several worked decisions. |
| 2. From Thought to Clear Sentence | 8–10 min | A focused lesson on syntax, specificity, grammar, and one read-aloud demonstration. |
| 3. Make the Writer’s Decisions | 11–13 min | Requires a full decision-by-decision example and a removal test. |
| 4. Build Movement from Cause and Choice | 12–15 min | Story logic needs contrast, causality, and a complete beat-map walk-through. |
| 5. Choose a Shape That Fits the Material | 10–12 min | Compares structures and shows when not to use them. |
| 6. Find, Check, and Organize Evidence | 12–15 min | Includes idea selection, a claim ledger, source limits, and a research-to-outline demo. |
| 7. Earn Attention, Then Pay It Back | 11–13 min | Includes hook mechanisms, loops, ethical boundaries, and a rewrite lab. |
| 8. Build a Human Voice | 10–12 min | A close autopsy of generic, corporate, and fake-confessional writing. |
| 9. Write for Breath, Rhythm, and Comprehension | 10–12 min | Demonstrates sentence and information pacing, holds, pronunciation, and read-aloud repair. |
| 10. Write for Image, Sound, and Edit | 10–12 min | Walks through one beat across script, picture, sound, and viewer experience. |
| 11. Change the Architecture, Not Just the Runtime | 11–13 min | Demonstrates compression and expansion while preserving independent payoffs. |
| 12. Brief-to-Script Workshop | 15–18 min | A complete guided workshop with decision points and a production handoff. |
| 13. Diagnose a Weak Script | 11–13 min | Rewrites need to show the diagnosis and the reason, not only the improved line. |
| 14. Revise in the Right Order | 11–13 min | The ten passes, feedback, scorecard, and change log are practiced end to end. |
| 15. Control Lens, Emphasis, and Omission | 12–15 min | Advanced craft is taught through one set of facts viewed three ways. |
| 16. Capstone Studio | 15–20 min | Guides the project from brief to final check and postmortem. |

The scripts contain roughly **18,700 spoken words**—about **2:05–2:25 of continuous narration** at 130–150 words per minute. Several lessons include demonstrations, comparison holds, and time to answer or write; a recorded course that includes those teaching moments should land closer to **2.5–3 hours**. Runtime estimates are planning targets, not a reason to stretch a lesson artificially.

## Projects that build on one another

1. **Project 1 — The idea card:** one audience, one question, one promise, and one honest piece of evidence.
2. **Project 2 — The seven-beat map:** a real change with cause, choice, and consequence.
3. **Project 3 — The researched outline:** claims traced to sources, a counterpoint, and an order that helps a beginner understand.
4. **Project 4 — The production draft:** timed VO, visual proof, sound/edit notes, and open verification items clearly marked.
5. **Project 5 — The capstone package:** long-form version, independent Short, reader test, revision record, rights/accuracy check, and postmortem.

## Four embedded workshops

1. **Idea-and-angle lab (Phases 1–2):** turn a broad subject into several distinct, evidence-backed video ideas.
2. **Hook lab (Phase 5):** write scene, demo, question, and direct-promise openings; choose by available proof.
3. **Brief-to-script workshop (Phase 9):** brief → research → beats → first draft → rewrite → production handoff.
4. **Autopsy-and-rewrite lab (Phase 10):** diagnose a deliberately weak sample, revise it, and explain each decision.

The capstone is a fifth, longer studio project rather than another reading exercise.

## Recommended case-study route

Use the real-video analyses after learning the relevant tool, not as a substitute for practice:

- **Kallaway, “How To Become A Master Storyteller”** after Lessons 4–5: trace cause/effect, sentence rhythm, examples, and the lesson’s progression.
- **Isaac, “How I Actually Make AI Voice Sound Real”** after Lessons 8–10: separate script, performance, tools, editing, and honest outcome.
- **Ali Abdaal, “How to Invest for Beginners”** after Lessons 5–6: watch how a beginner question can precede terminology. This is a structure study, **not** financial guidance.

Read the [case-study library](CASE-STUDY-LIBRARY.md) before using any real-video analysis. The source timecodes are approximate and derived from captions; verify the original video.

## Progress gates

- **After Phase 1:** explain the difference between topic, question, angle, thesis, and promise; write one clear, concrete paragraph.
- **After Phase 3:** map a real change and defend the structure chosen for it.
- **After Phase 5:** show where the promise begins to be paid and close every meaningful loop.
- **After Phase 8:** deliver a Short that works without the long video and a long outline that earns its time.
- **After Phase 10:** make revisions in order and explain what changed, why, and what evidence supports the change.
- **Graduation:** a skeptical viewer can tell what is observed, inferred, and unknown; an editor can produce the script without guessing; the learner can defend the choices without pointing to a formula.

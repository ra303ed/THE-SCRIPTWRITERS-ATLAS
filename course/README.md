# The Scriptwriter’s Atlas — Professional Video Scriptwriting Course

**From first idea to production-ready script**  
A practical course in English, built from the supplied Atlas rather than arranged page by page.

> **Course language:** Every lesson, example, exercise, checkpoint, and feedback tool is in English. The only Arabic teaching material is the final recording script, linked at the end of the course.

## Start here

1. Read the [course architecture and learning roadmap](ROADMAP.md).
2. Work through the 12 phases in order. Each phase begins with **You Are Here / You Are Learning / By the End** and ends with **Mastered / Next**.
3. Do the exercise before reading its check or model answer. Use the [compact workbook](WORKBOOK.md) to keep your work in one place.
4. Complete the [capstone](phases/12-capstone.md) with a real topic, honest evidence, and a production package.
5. Only after the English course, open the [final Arabic voiceover lesson](FINAL-ARABIC-VOICEOVER.md).

## Course architecture

- **12 progressive phases**
- **16 core lessons**
- **4 embedded workshops** (idea-and-angle lab, hook lab, brief-to-script workshop, autopsy-and-rewrite lab)
- **3 recommended case-study deep dives** used to model close analysis
- **1 professional capstone**: a long-form production package plus a self-contained Short
- **Reference tools**: ten reusable workbook sheets, a compact glossary, a revision system, a scorecard, and a 30-day practice plan
- **13 optional real-video case studies**: useful for transfer practice, not prerequisites

The course is designed around a repeatable loop:

**UNDERSTAND → CHOOSE → WRITE → TEST → REVISE → PRODUCE**

The aim is not to memorize a structure. It is to make and explain the right decision for this viewer, this idea, this evidence, and this production.

## What was merged, compressed, and retained

The source Atlas contains 41 distinct HTML pages in a ZIP (the extra `index.html` repeats the home page). It mixes foundations, professional decisions, workshops, three long original scripts, six short original scripts, a 13-video analysis library, a workbook, reference pages, and advanced craft. The course keeps those distinctions where they help learning; it does not turn each source page into a required lesson.

### Merged into the core

- The early “12 writing decisions” are threaded through the idea, mindset, research, workflow, and revision lessons instead of repeated as a separate rule list.
- “From Thought to Sentence,” “English for Writers,” “Human Voice,” and “Pacing” are taught as a progression: clear thought, clear sentence, owned voice, then spoken performance.
- Story engine, cause-and-effect transitions, setup/payoff, open loops, and structure selection are combined so a learner sees both **how a beat moves** and **when a format fits**.
- The repeated research, audience, angle, thesis, evidence, and outline guidance is taught once as a hands-on route from question to evidence-backed outline.
- The editing lab, autopsy library, ten-pass revision, scorecard, and external-reader tests become a diagnosis lesson plus a revision lesson.
- The workshop’s brief → research → outline → hook → draft → fact-check → production cycle is rebuilt as a working exercise, not passive reference.

### Essential versus optional

**Core course:** idea selection; audience, angle, promise, thesis; clear English; evidence; story and structure; hooks and ethical retention; human voice; spoken pacing; visual/audio decisions; long/short architecture; workflow; autopsy; revision; feedback; and the capstone.

**Recommended deep dives:** Kallaway’s storytelling lesson, Isaac’s AI-voice tutorial, and Ali Abdaal’s beginner investing explainer. They are studied for observable writing decisions—not copied phrases, personal histories, financial recommendations, or assumed creator intent. The [case-study library](CASE-STUDY-LIBRARY.md) explains how to study all 13 analyses without making all of them mandatory.

**Optional case studies:** the other ten real-video analyses. Use them when you need another example in a format or topic area.

**Reference:** the six complete short scripts, the other long original scripts, glossary, desk cards, extra exercises, and 30-day plan. One original-script walkthrough is integrated into the core; the source originals remain available in the archive.

## Source fidelity and limits

The supplied ZIP is the primary source. The transformation keeps its central practices: write decisions rather than fill-in formulas; use evidence and state limits; build movement through cause, choice, and consequence; pay off the promise; write for voice and image; distinguish real-video analysis from original scripts and workshops; and revise in separate passes.

The source’s real-video notes are paraphrased readings of captions with approximate timecodes. They cannot establish private intent, exact audience response, or why a creator made a choice. The course teaches that distinction explicitly. Check the linked original video before making a claim about its visuals, performance, or editing. Financial, health, platform, product, and rights details can change; verify them before publication.

The new course examples are teaching examples, not claims that a fictional production test happened to the course author or learner. Labels such as **[VERIFY]**, **[SHOOT]**, and **[DEMO]** are kept where they prevent a polished script from becoming a false one.

## Voiceover scripts and audio

Every core lesson includes a complete, conversational English voiceover script designed to teach rather than read the page. Runtime estimates are planning estimates; recording pace, pauses, and visual holds change the actual length. The scripts are ready to record. Generated audio is included for **Lessons 1–8** (Phase 1 Lessons 1–2, Phase 2 Lesson 3, Phase 3 Lessons 4–5, Phase 4 Lesson 6, Phase 5 Lesson 7, and Phase 6 Lesson 8), split into numbered MP3 parts with playlists because of per-clip generation limits. The remaining eight English voiceover scripts are complete; their audio can be generated in later batches. See [audio files and playlists](audio/README.md).
